How to Treat Rural Domestic Sewage with Integrated Treatment Equipment?

Author: Admin Published: Jun 11, 2021 Category: Technical Articles Views: 1,258

Wuhan integrated sewage treatment equipment - how to treat rural domestic sewage? Most rural areas have the problem of discharging sewage directly into local water bodies without treatment, causing serious pollution to the environment. With increasing environmental awareness and stricter regulations, finding an effective rural sewage treatment solution has become a priority for many communities.

Integrated sewage treatment equipment is specifically designed for rural domestic sewage treatment. It combines multiple treatment processes including anaerobic, anoxic, and aerobic biological treatment into a single compact unit. The equipment is buried underground, with only the control panel visible above ground, minimizing visual impact and odor emissions.

The treatment process of integrated sewage treatment equipment for rural areas includes the following steps:

1. Pretreatment: Sewage enters a grid chamber to remove large solids and debris, protecting downstream equipment.

2. Anaerobic Treatment: The sewage flows into the anaerobic chamber where anaerobic bacteria break down organic matter, reducing COD and BOD levels.

3. Anoxic Treatment: In the anoxic chamber, denitrifying bacteria convert nitrates into nitrogen gas, effectively removing nitrogen from the wastewater.

4. Aerobic Treatment: The aerobic chamber uses activated sludge or biofilm processes to further decompose organic pollutants under oxygen-rich conditions.

5. Sedimentation: A secondary sedimentation tank removes suspended solids and biological sludge from the treated water.

6. Disinfection: Finally, the treated water passes through a disinfection chamber (using UV or chlorine) to eliminate pathogens before discharge.

The advantages of integrated sewage treatment equipment for rural areas include: compact design requiring minimal land area, low energy consumption, easy operation and maintenance, stable treatment performance, and compliance with Class I-A discharge standards. The equipment can be customized based on population size, water quality, and site conditions.
