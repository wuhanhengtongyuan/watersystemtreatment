China's Sludge Treatment Rate Has Not Exceeded 20% in 5 Years

Author: Admin Published: Apr 21, 2018 Category: Industry News Views: 587

With the promulgation of the "Water Ten Plan" (Action Plan for Prevention and Treatment of Water Pollution), the state has set requirements for sludge treatment facilities and disposal rates. However, data shows that China's sludge treatment rate has remained below 20% for five years, highlighting a significant gap in the country's environmental protection infrastructure.

Sludge is the main byproduct of urban sewage treatment. For every ton of sewage treated, approximately 0.5-1 kg of dry sludge is generated. With the rapid expansion of sewage treatment capacity in China - urban sewage treatment rate has exceeded 90% - the amount of sludge generated has increased dramatically, creating an urgent need for proper sludge treatment and disposal.

The current situation of sludge treatment in China faces several challenges:

1. Low Treatment Rate: Statistics show that less than 20% of urban sludge in China undergoes proper treatment before disposal. The remaining 80% is simply landfilled, dumped, or used in agriculture without adequate stabilization, causing secondary pollution.

2. Inadequate Facilities: Many sewage treatment plants lack dedicated sludge treatment facilities. Even when sludge treatment equipment exists, it often operates below capacity due to technical limitations or budget constraints.

3. High Moisture Content: Most sludge in China has a moisture content of 80% or higher after mechanical dewatering, making it unsuitable for direct landfill or incineration. Further drying is needed but is often not implemented.

4. Technical Challenges: Sludge treatment involves complex processes including thickening, dewatering, stabilization, drying, and final disposal. The selection of appropriate technology depends on sludge characteristics, local conditions, and disposal standards.

5. Funding Issues: Sludge treatment requires significant capital investment and operating costs. Many municipalities prioritize sewage treatment over sludge treatment, leading to underfunded sludge treatment facilities.

The main sludge treatment and disposal methods include:

- Sanitary Landfill: The most common method in China, but it occupies valuable land resources and risks groundwater contamination.

- Incineration: Effective for volume reduction and pathogen elimination, but requires high investment and may cause air pollution if not properly managed.

- Composting/Land Application: Uses sludge as a soil conditioner or fertilizer, but concerns about heavy metals and pathogens limit its widespread use.

- Drying and Resource Recovery: Dried sludge can be used as a fuel source in cement kilns or power plants, providing energy recovery.

To address the sludge treatment challenge, the government has strengthened regulatory requirements and increased funding support. New policies require all cities to build sludge treatment facilities and achieve a treatment rate of over 90% by the end of the 13th Five-Year Plan period.
