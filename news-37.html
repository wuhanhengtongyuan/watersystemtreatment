How Much Does Pig Farm Sewage Treatment Equipment Cost?

Author: Admin Published: May 20, 2021 Category: Technical Articles Views: 1,102

How much does pig farm sewage treatment equipment cost? Pig farms often have the impression of being smelly, but with growing environmental awareness, more and more pig farms are seeking solutions to treat their wastewater. The cost of pig farm sewage treatment equipment depends on multiple factors.

Pig farm wastewater is characterized by high concentrations of organic matter, ammonia nitrogen, suspended solids, and pathogens. It typically has a COD of 5000-15000 mg/L, BOD of 2000-8000 mg/L, and ammonia nitrogen of 500-2000 mg/L, making it one of the most challenging types of wastewater to treat.

The main factors affecting the cost of pig farm sewage treatment equipment include:

1. Farm Scale: The size of the pig farm directly determines the daily wastewater volume. A farm with 1,000 pigs generates approximately 30-50 tons of wastewater per day, while a farm with 10,000 pigs may generate 300-500 tons/day.

2. Wastewater Characteristics: The pollution load of pig farm wastewater varies depending on the farming method (dry manure cleaning vs. water flushing). Dry manure systems produce less wastewater but higher concentration, while water flushing systems produce more volume at lower concentration.

3. Treatment Process: Pig farm wastewater treatment typically involves solid-liquid separation, anaerobic digestion (biogas production), anoxic/aerobic biological treatment, and disinfection. The choice of process affects both equipment and operating costs.

4. Discharge Standards: Different regions have different discharge standards. Meeting stricter standards requires more advanced treatment processes and higher investment.

5. Biogas Utilization: If the farm plans to utilize biogas generated from anaerobic treatment, additional equipment for biogas collection, purification, and utilization is needed, which adds to the total cost but provides energy recovery benefits.

6. Resource Recovery: The treated wastewater can be used for irrigation, and the separated solid waste can be processed into organic fertilizer, creating additional value and offsetting some treatment costs.

The typical treatment process for pig farm wastewater includes: solid-liquid separation, equalization, anaerobic digestion (UASB or CSTR), anoxic tank for denitrification, aerobic tank (A/O or SBR), secondary sedimentation, and disinfection. The treated water can meet irrigation water standards or discharge standards.
